01 / Context & direction

A practical direction for a tight timeline.

RightCapital is a financial planning platform for advisors. Iris brings AI into the current client plan to identify data issues, explain cash flow and suggest combinations of planning adjustments.

The feature concept existed, but the interaction model still needed to be defined. Three designers explored separate demos. The other directions drew more heavily on competitors and proposed larger changes. Drawing on my long experience with the product, I focused on small, feasible adjustments to existing patterns.

Reuse familiar workflows. Keep the implementation scope focused.

With delivery time limited, feasibility was a central consideration. The CEO selected my direction. I designed the suggested questions, data authorization, result presentation and connections to existing editing flows, then handed over an HTML prototype before leaving the company.

Product context: RightCapital Q2 2026 update. Public product materials explain the context; they are not personal research or evidence of design impact.

02 / From finding to fixing

Let advisors fix the issue where they find it.

Editing entry points were scattered across the product. Advisors could struggle to find the right field, and a change in one place could affect a small detail on a distant page without making the connection obvious.

This was a recurring problem I understood from working on the product over time. I proposed connecting an AI finding directly to the relevant data card and existing edit drawer, so advisors could act in context.

Connect the answer to an existing action.

For an issue such as “Retirement expense not entered,” the relevant data card opens a second edit drawer. Advisors can reach the field from the AI result instead of searching across pages.

01 / IdentifyRead the issue card
02 / LocateSelect the relevant data
03 / ActOpen the existing edit drawer

Short titles and explanations make the findings easier to scan. Color labels include written priority levels, while the data cards reuse the platform’s visual language.

The design aimed to reduce searching and page switching. Time savings were not measured.

03 / Compare plans

Show the improvement and what it requires.

A higher success rate only becomes useful when advisors can see which assumptions need to change to achieve it.

Success probability is an existing core metric calculated by RightCapital’s planning algorithm. My contribution was the presentation: consistent Plan A, B and C cards pair the expected change in success rate with the parameters each option requires.

Expected resultProposed success rate

The cards support comparison rather than navigation to another page. I also explored expanding longer explanations downward; the detailed expansion rules were still open at handoff.

04 / Data authorization

Ask for access, then continue the task.

The project required explicit consent before AI could access client data. Authorization needed to support the task without forcing advisors to start again.

I placed the access control and connection state in the drawer. Advisors could enable access first or ask a question. When a question required client data, Iris would request authorization and continue the original question after confirmation.

The CEO explicitly endorsed this continuity in the flow.

05 / Getting started

Make the useful tasks visible.

An empty input gives users freedom, but does little to explain where the product is most useful.

I surfaced the questions prioritized for the first phase, giving advisors a starting point in plan improvement, data checking and cash-flow analysis.

06 / Review & handoff

A selected direction, with clear next steps.

I presented the prototype to engineering and product colleagues. Engineering raised no design objections and moved the discussion toward implementation.

Internal feedback led to an output-length constraint.

A product colleague pointed out that the narrow drawer made answer length especially important. Long responses could become inconvenient to read and make advisors less willing to read. The team decided to limit output length.

This was a concrete decision from internal review. A specific limit, its implementation and user validation are not claimed here.

My contribution ended at handoff.

I completed the core interaction direction and HTML demo, then handed them to a colleague before leaving. I did not participate in the final release.

Iris launched in June 2026. The public version retained the right-side drawer, preset tasks and free-form questions, while data access and result details evolved. Subsequent additions are outside my contribution.

The work shown here is my design-stage prototype. Internal review supported design and implementation decisions; it was not a formal usability test.
